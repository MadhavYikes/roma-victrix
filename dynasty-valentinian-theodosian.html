Dynastic formation

Valentinian I, Theodosius I and the late western court.

Political structure

Valentinian-Theodosian rulers relied on combinations of biological family, adoption, marriage alliances, military backing, senatorial recognition and court institutions.

Rulers

364–375

Valentinian I

Western emperor known for frontier defense and administrative reform.

364–378

Valens

Eastern emperor killed at Adrianople.

367–383

Gratian

Western emperor who elevated Theodosius and faced military pressure.

375–392

Valentinian II

Child western emperor under powerful generals and court factions.

Succession

Succession crises reveal the limits of dynastic labels. Co-emperorship, adoption, military proclamation and civil war repeatedly changed the line of succession.

Material culture

Coins and monuments helped present dynastic continuity through portraits of emperors, heirs, wives and children.
