Dynastic formation

Nerva through Commodus; adoption, co-rule and succession.

Political structure

Nerva-Antonine rulers relied on combinations of biological family, adoption, marriage alliances, military backing, senatorial recognition and court institutions.

Rulers

96–98

Nerva

Elder senator who stabilized the succession after Domitian's assassination.

98–117

Trajan

Empire's greatest territorial expansion; Dacian and Parthian campaigns.

117–138

Hadrian

Frontier consolidation, travel, law and monumental architecture.

138–161

Antoninus Pius

Long, comparatively peaceful reign and extensive legal and administrative activity.

161–169

Lucius Verus

Co-emperor with Marcus Aurelius; eastern war against Parthia.

161–180

Marcus Aurelius

Philosopher-emperor; Marcomannic wars and Antonine plague.

180–192

Commodus

Son of Marcus Aurelius; ended the Antonine succession and was assassinated.

Succession

Succession crises reveal the limits of dynastic labels. Co-emperorship, adoption, military proclamation and civil war repeatedly changed the line of succession.

Material culture

Coins and monuments helped present dynastic continuity through portraits of emperors, heirs, wives and children.
