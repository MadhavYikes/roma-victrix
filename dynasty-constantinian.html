Dynastic formation

Constantine and his sons, followed by Julian.

Political structure

Constantinian rulers relied on combinations of biological family, adoption, marriage alliances, military backing, senatorial recognition and court institutions.

Rulers

308–324

Licinius

Co-emperor with Constantine until defeated in the final civil war.

306–337

Constantine I

Reunified the empire and founded Constantinople; first emperor to favor Christianity openly.

337–340

Constantine II

Son of Constantine I and western co-emperor.

337–361

Constantius II

Longest-reigning son of Constantine; fought Persia and multiple western usurpers.

337–350

Constans

Ruler of Italy, Africa and the western Balkans until overthrown.

361–363

Julian

Last pagan emperor; Persian campaign ended in his death.

Succession

Succession crises reveal the limits of dynastic labels. Co-emperorship, adoption, military proclamation and civil war repeatedly changed the line of succession.

Material culture

Coins and monuments helped present dynastic continuity through portraits of emperors, heirs, wives and children.
