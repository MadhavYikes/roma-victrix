Claimants & Usurpers

Imperial power was contested repeatedly. This index separates formal emperors from people who claimed or exercised imperial authority without becoming universally recognized.

407–411

Constantine III

British-origin military claimant who controlled much of Gaul and Spain for a time.

409–411

Constans II

Son of Constantine III and co-ruler in the western usurpation.

409–410; 414–415

Priscus Attalus

Twice proclaimed emperor by the Visigoths against Honorius.

411–413

Jovinus

Gallic aristocrat proclaimed emperor after Constantine III's fall.

475–476

Romulus Augustulus

Last emperor to rule from Italy before Odoacer's deposition.

68

Clodius Macer

Legate in Africa who revolted during Nero's final year.

69

Aulus Vitellius Germanicus?

Alias preserved for source indexing; main profile is Vitellius.

175

Avidius Cassius

Eastern general proclaimed emperor after claiming Marcus Aurelius had died.

193–194

Pescennius Niger

Eastern governor and rival to Septimius Severus.

193–197

Clodius Albinus

British governor and rival to Septimius Severus.

c. 253–254

Uranius

Eastern claimant known mainly from coinage and fragmentary evidence.

260

Ingenuus

Danubian military commander who revolted against Gallienus.

260

Regalianus

Danubian claimant known from coins and literary fragments.

260–269

Postumus

Founder of the Gallic Empire in the western provinces.

269

Laelianus

Brief Gallic claimant after Postumus.

269

Marius

Very brief Gallic emperor between Laelianus and Victorinus.

269–271

Victorinus

Gallic emperor who maintained the western breakaway state.

271–274

Tetricus I

Last Gallic emperor, defeated by Aurelian.

270–272

Vaballathus

Palmyrene ruler associated with Zenobia's eastern breakaway state.

272

Zenobia

Palmyrene ruler who challenged Aurelian's authority.

268

Aureolus

Military commander who revolted against Gallienus and Claudius II.

271–272

Domitianus

Short-lived claimant in Egypt during Aurelian's reign.

273

Firmus

Egyptian rebel associated with the suppression of Palmyrene resistance.

280–281

Proculus

Rebel claimant during Probus' reign.

280–281

Bonosus

Rhine commander who revolted against Probus.

285–286

Amandus

Bagaudic claimant in Gaul.

285–286

Aelianus

Bagaudic claimant associated with Amandus.

286–293

Carausius

Naval commander who ruled Britain and parts of Gaul.

293–296

Allectus

Succeeded Carausius in Britain before Constantius Chlorus reconquered the province.

296–297

Domitius Domitianus

Egyptian rebel against Diocletian.

297–298

Achilleus

Egyptian rebel who continued resistance after Domitius Domitianus.

308–310

Lucius Domitius Alexander

African governor who revolted against Maxentius.

306–312

Maxentius' rival Constantine?

Cross-reference only; main profile is Constantine I.

350–353

Magnentius

Usurper who defeated Constans and controlled the West.

350–353

Decentius

Brother of Magnentius and Caesar in the western revolt.

350

Nepotian

Brief urban claimant in Rome during Magnentius' revolt.

350

Vetranio

Balkan military commander briefly elevated as emperor.

355

Silvanus

Frankish-born general who was proclaimed emperor at Cologne.

365–366

Procopius

Relative of Julian who revolted against Valens in Constantinople.

366

Marcellus

Brief claimant associated with Procopius' revolt.

383–388

Magnus Maximus

British commander who became western emperor and was defeated by Theodosius I.

392–394

Eugenius

Civilian usurper backed by Arbogast and defeated by Theodosius I.

392–394

Arbogast

General who elevated Eugenius against Theodosius I.

397–398

Gildo

African commander whose revolt threatened the grain supply to Rome.

406–407

Marcus

One of the short-lived emperors elevated in Britain.

412–413

Sebastianus

Co-claimant associated with Jovinus in Gaul.

413

Heraclianus

African military commander who revolted against Honorius.

409–411

Maximus

Son of Gerontius and claimant in Hispania.

423–425

Ioannes

Senior official who seized the western throne after Honorius' death.
