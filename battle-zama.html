Zama

Scipio Africanus defeated Hannibal and ended Carthage's ability to contest Roman supremacy.

ContentsBackgroundCommanders and forcesTerrain and battleOutcomeConsequencesSources
Background

The battle occurred during Second Punic War. The strategic context, alliances and political stakes shaped the campaign before the armies met.

Commanders and forces

The principal commanders and formations should be studied alongside the wider campaign. Exact troop numbers in ancient sources are often uncertain or rhetorical, so Roma Aeterna distinguishes securely attested information from estimates.

Terrain and battle

Terrain, logistics, formation, cavalry, missile troops and command decisions shaped the engagement at Zama. A battle should not be reduced to a single tactical diagram: the surrounding operational campaign often explains the result.

Outcome

Scipio Africanus defeated Hannibal and ended Carthage's ability to contest Roman supremacy.

Consequences

The consequences extended beyond the battlefield, affecting succession, territory, diplomacy, military doctrine and political legitimacy. The importance of Zama is best measured by what changed afterward.

Sources

Ancient histories, inscriptions, archaeology and battlefield geography are the principal evidence. Where accounts disagree, the disagreement is part of the historical problem rather than something to conceal.
