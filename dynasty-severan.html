Dynastic formation

Septimius Severus through Severus Alexander.

Political structure

Severan rulers relied on combinations of biological family, adoption, marriage alliances, military backing, senatorial recognition and court institutions.

Rulers

193–211

Septimius Severus

Founder of the Severan dynasty; civil-war victor and frontier emperor.

211–217

Caracalla

Severan ruler associated with the Constitutio Antoniniana and major military campaigns.

209–211

Geta

Younger son of Septimius Severus and co-emperor with Caracalla.

217–218

Macrinus

First emperor not from the senatorial elite to reach power without prior senatorial career.

217–218

Diadumenian

Son of Macrinus and briefly co-emperor.

218–222

Elagabalus

Young Severan emperor associated with the cult of Elagabal and intense court politics.

222–235

Severus Alexander

Last Severan emperor; killed by his own troops during the German war.

Succession

Succession crises reveal the limits of dynastic labels. Co-emperorship, adoption, military proclamation and civil war repeatedly changed the line of succession.

Material culture

Coins and monuments helped present dynastic continuity through portraits of emperors, heirs, wives and children.
