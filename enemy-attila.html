Attila

Origins and political structure

Attila developed within the political and geographic environment of hunnic ruler. Roman authors often described opponents using categories such as barbarian, king or rebel, but archaeological and external evidence can provide a more complex picture.

Why Rome came into conflict

The conflict with Rome arose from a combination of strategic geography, alliance systems, trade, client kingship, migration, expansion and internal Roman politics. The phrase 'Hunnic Wars' describes the principal Roman confrontation associated with this article.

Major campaigns

The military history of Attila belongs to a longer sequence of campaigns rather than a single battle. Commanders, supply, terrain, fortifications and local alliances were decisive. See the battle index for individual engagements.

Military system

Roman armies adapted their tactics and organization to the opponent. Cavalry, siegecraft, naval power, fortified frontiers and diplomacy were often as important as battlefield infantry.

Outcome and consequences

The relationship between Attila and Rome changed the political geography of the Mediterranean and frontier zones. Outcomes could include annexation, client kingdoms, negotiated settlements, migration, destruction of cities or the creation of new military frontiers.

Sources and archaeology

Evidence includes Roman literary accounts, inscriptions, coins, fortifications, burial archaeology and material culture. Roman narratives are particularly important but should be checked against independent archaeological evidence.

Legacy
