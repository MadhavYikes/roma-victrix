Imperial Dynasties

Family, adoption and political succession. Dynasties are useful organizing categories, but Roman emperorship was never governed by a single hereditary law.

27 BC–68 AD

Julio-Claudian

Augustus, Tiberius, Caligula, Claudius and Nero.

69–96 AD

Flavian

Vespasian, Titus and Domitian.

96–192 AD

Nerva-Antonine

Nerva through Commodus; adoption, co-rule and succession.

193–235 AD

Severan

Septimius Severus through Severus Alexander.

284–324 AD

Tetrarchy

Diocletian, Maximian, Galerius, Constantius and their rivals.

306–363 AD

Constantinian

Constantine and his sons, followed by Julian.

364–455 AD

Valentinian-Theodosian

Valentinian I, Theodosius I and the late western court.

717–802 AD

Isaurian

Leo III through Constantine VI and Irene.

820–867 AD

Amorian

Michael II through Michael III.

867–1056 AD

Macedonian

Basil I through the Macedonian succession.

1081–1185 AD

Komnenian

Alexios I through Andronikos I.

1185–1204 AD

Angelos

Isaac II, Alexios III, Alexios IV and the crisis of 1204.

1205–1261 AD

Laskarid

Nicaean Roman emperors preserving imperial continuity after 1204.

1259–1453 AD

Palaiologan

Michael VIII through Constantine XI.
