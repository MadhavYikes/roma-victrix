Enemies of Rome

A comprehensive enemy index. Rome's expansion produced recurring conflict with rival states, peoples, kings, federations and commanders. Each entry is treated as an article rather than a thumbnail.

Phoenician/North African state

Carthage

Roman–Carthaginian struggle for Mediterranean supremacy.

Carthaginian commander

Hannibal Barca

The commander whose invasion of Italy produced Cannae and the greatest Roman military crisis of the Republic.

Hellenistic kingdom

Macedon

Rome's wars with the Antigonid kingdom reshaped the eastern Mediterranean.

Hellenistic empire

Seleucid Empire

Rome's intervention in the Greek East brought it into conflict with the Seleucid monarchy.

King of Pontus

Mithridates VI

A major eastern opponent whose wars forced Rome to reorganize Asia Minor.

Iranian empire

Parthian Empire

Rome's principal eastern rival before the Sasanian dynasty.

Iranian empire

Sasanian Empire

The great eastern rival of the late Roman Empire from the third to seventh centuries.

Kingdom north of the Danube

Dacian Kingdom

Decebalus' kingdom was conquered by Trajan after two major wars.

Gallic Arvernian leader

Vercingetorix

United major Gallic forces against Caesar and surrendered after the siege of Alesia.

Cheruscan leader

Arminius

Germanic commander associated with the defeat of Varus in the Teutoburg Forest.

Britannic Iceni queen

Boudica

Led the major revolt of 60/61 against Roman rule in Britain.

Eastern breakaway state

Palmyrene Empire

Zenobia and Vaballathus challenged Roman authority before Aurelian's reconquest.

Germanic peoples

Goths

Repeatedly fought Rome from the third century through late antiquity.

Visigothic king

Alaric

Led the Visigoths into Italy and the sack of Rome in 410.

Germanic kingdom in Africa

Vandals

Captured North Africa and sacked Rome in 455.

Hunnic ruler

Attila

Led the Hunnic confederation against Roman territory in the fifth century.

Germanic confederation

Franks

Expanded in northern Gaul and became central to the post-Roman West.

Germanic confederation

Alamanni

Frequent opponents of Roman armies along the upper Rhine.

Iranian-speaking steppe peoples

Sarmatians

Mounted peoples repeatedly involved in Roman frontier warfare.

Caucasian kingdom

Persian Armenia

Armenia was a strategic buffer contested by Rome and Persia.
