Julio-Claudian

Dynastic formation

Augustus, Tiberius, Caligula, Claudius and Nero.

Political structure

Julio-Claudian rulers relied on combinations of biological family, adoption, marriage alliances, military backing, senatorial recognition and court institutions.

Rulers

27 BC–14 AD

Augustus

Founding princeps; reorganized the Roman state after the civil wars.

14–37

Tiberius

Second emperor; consolidated Augustus' system and managed difficult frontier and succession politics.

37–41

Caligula

Third Julio-Claudian emperor; remembered for court politics, projects and a violent end.

41–54

Claudius

Administrator and conqueror of Britain.

54–68

Nero

Last Julio-Claudian; patron of spectacle and arts, defeated by provincial revolt.

Succession

Succession crises reveal the limits of dynastic labels. Co-emperorship, adoption, military proclamation and civil war repeatedly changed the line of succession.

Material culture

Coins and monuments helped present dynastic continuity through portraits of emperors, heirs, wives and children.
