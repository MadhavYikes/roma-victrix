Geography

Grain, Alexandria, papyri and the Nile made Egypt strategically exceptional.

Roman annexation

The province became part of the Roman political system through conquest, diplomacy, inheritance or reorganization. The exact process varied by region.

Administration

Provincial administration combined governors, imperial officials, municipal councils, local elites, tax systems and military commands.

Cities and infrastructure

Urban centers, roads, forts, temples, baths and water systems connected the province to the wider imperial economy.

Military

Frontier provinces could host legions and auxiliary units, while interior provinces relied more heavily on municipal administration and road networks.

Economy

Agriculture, taxation, mining, trade and local production tied the province to Mediterranean and continental markets.

Late Roman changes

Late imperial reforms divided many older provinces into smaller units and grouped them into dioceses and prefectures.

Archaeology

Inscriptions, coins, buildings, ceramics, roads, forts and burials provide evidence for life in Aegyptus.
