Roman Provinces

Administrative geography. Province names, boundaries and legal status changed repeatedly, especially during the reforms of Diocletian and his successors.

Egypt

Aegyptus

Grain, Alexandria, papyri and the Nile made Egypt strategically exceptional.

North Africa

Africa Proconsularis

The African provinces were central to grain supply, agriculture and Mediterranean trade.

Western Anatolia

Asia

One of the wealthiest eastern provinces with major Greek cities and sanctuaries.

Britain

Britannia

A frontier province organized around military bases, roads, towns and Hadrian’s Wall.

Gaul

Gallia

A large western region progressively integrated into Roman provincial administration.

Iberian Peninsula

Hispania

Romanization, mining, agriculture and major provincial cities shaped the peninsula.

Levant

Syria

A wealthy eastern region and major frontier zone facing Parthia and Persia.

Danube north of the river

Dacia

Trajan’s conquest created a Roman province later abandoned under Aurelian.

Lower Danube

Moesia

A militarized Danubian region with major forts and legions.

Middle Danube

Pannonia

A strategic military province central to the later imperial succession system.

Southern Levant

Arabia Petraea

Created under Trajan and linked desert routes with the Roman road network.

Southern Levant

Judea

A politically and religiously significant province with repeated revolts.

Balkans

Macedonia

A major route between the Adriatic, Aegean and eastern Mediterranean.

Greece

Achaea

A province centered on the Greek cities and sanctuaries of the southern Balkans.

Central Anatolia

Cappadocia

A major eastern military province facing Armenia and Persia.
