Roman Family

Definition and scope

Household structure, marriage, adoption, inheritance and patronage were central to Roman social organization. The subject developed over centuries, so Roman practice in the Republic, Principate, late Empire and eastern Roman period should not be treated as identical.

Origins

The foundations of roman family reach into the Roman Republic and earlier Mediterranean societies. Institutions were repeatedly adapted as Rome expanded into Italy, the Greek East, North Africa and the provinces.

Imperial period

During the imperial period, roman family became increasingly connected to the emperor, provincial administration and the army. Local communities retained substantial variation while sharing Roman legal and cultural frameworks.

Regional variation

There was no single uniform Roman experience. Italy, Gaul, Hispania, Africa, Egypt, Syria, Asia Minor, the Balkans and the eastern Mediterranean differed in language, economy, urbanism and local institutions.

Evidence

The subject is reconstructed from inscriptions, papyri, coins, archaeological remains and literary sources. Material evidence is particularly important for testing elite literary narratives.

Late Roman transformation

The third through sixth centuries brought major changes in military organization, taxation, provincial administration, religion and urban life. Eastern Roman institutions continued to evolve after the western court disappeared.
